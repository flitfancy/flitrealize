# 4.1 Manufacturing files and prototype ordering

Read this reference when the PCB is complete and the project needs export, review, or preparation for a prototype order.

This stage turns the current PCB source into a matching set of files that can be submitted to a board or assembly house.

## Confirm the current candidate

Before export, confirm:

- the saved PCB source;
- matching schematic, BOM, and board version;
- board outline, layer count, thickness, copper weight, and major manufacturing requirements;
- current DRC state;
- bare-board or PCBA scope;
- quantity and assembly variant.

Accepted prototype limitations may remain, but schematic, PCB, BOM, and placement files from different versions must not be mixed.

When the source is still changing, save it first and export the complete set from that same version.

## Bare-board files

A bare-board order usually needs:

- Gerber;
- PTH and NPTH drill files;
- slots and routed edges;
- board outline;
- required fabrication notes.

Check:

- copper, solder mask, silkscreen, and mechanical layers;
- closed outline and dimensions;
- holes, slots, and internal routing;
- top and bottom orientation;
- current copper pours;
- mask, paste, and via-covering requirements;
- width, spacing, drill, and edge clearances;
- extra outlines, abandoned layers, and stale-version content.

File count does not prove a correct export.

## PCBA files

PCBA also needs:

- a controlled BOM;
- CPL or placement coordinates;
- DNP and assembly variants;
- top/bottom side information;
- polarity and pin 1;
- required assembly notes.

For the BOM, check:

- reference designator;
- value and role;
- manufacturer and exact MPN, or complete generic specification;
- package;
- purchasing code for this build;
- quantity;
- DNP state;
- currently allowed substitute range.

For the CPL, check:

- references agree with the BOM;
- X/Y coordinates;
- top and bottom side;
- rotation;
- origin;
- polarity and orientation for asymmetric parts.

Choose the order MPN from already accepted parts or substitute ranges. Do not rebuild electrical equivalence during ordering.

## Manufacturing preview

Use the current board-house or assembler preview to inspect:

- outline, dimensions, and layer count;
- copper and drilling;
- slots and shaped geometry;
- solder mask and silkscreen;
- top and bottom orientation;
- missing parts;
- polarity and pin 1;
- placement rotation;
- DFM notices and automatic modifications.

When a difference appears, determine whether it belongs to source design, export configuration, or fabricator interpretation, then correct the owning source.

## Preserve a manufacturing candidate

Source, outputs, and review results for one candidate should remain identifiable as a set.

A project may use:

```text
manufacturing/
`-- <candidate-id>/
    |-- manifest.md
    |-- pcb/
    `-- pcba/
```

`manifest.md` records:

- board name and version;
- candidate identifier or generation time;
- EDA source;
- Gerber, drill, BOM, and CPL;
- bare-board or PCBA scope;
- main manufacturing parameters;
- known limitations;
- fabricator preview or review result.

Generate a new candidate after a design change. Do not silently overwrite manufacturing files that were already accepted.

## Update the project manuscript

Under section 4.x, record:

- current manufacturing candidate;
- matching source;
- Gerber, drill, BOM, and CPL;
- bare-board or PCBA scope;
- current fabricator configuration;
- preview and review result;
- known limitations;
- current order state.

The manuscript keeps conclusions and entry points, not copies of the manufacturing package.

## Prototype ordering

Before ordering, confirm:

- the reviewed candidate is selected;
- fabricator options agree with the candidate;
- quantity, thickness, copper weight, finish, color, and special processes are correct;
- PCBA parts, substitutions, DNPs, assembly sides, and quantities are correct;
- quotations or out-of-stock substitutions have not introduced unreviewed parts;
- the bring-up and test plan is clear.

Generating and reviewing manufacturing files belongs to this stage. Submitting an order, paying, reserving stock, or deducting inventory requires user authorization for that action.

## Finish this stage

A ready prototype candidate has:

- a manufacturing package matched to the current saved source;
- reviewed Gerber and drill files;
- the BOM, CPL, and assembly information needed for PCBA;
- the current fabricator preview or equivalent review;
- the actual part identities used for this order;
- known limitations and prototype checks;
- an updated manuscript and current handoff.

When hardware arrives, read [5.1-prototype-validation.md](5.1-prototype-validation.md).
