# 4.1 Manufacturing files and prototype ordering

Read this reference when PCB work is complete and the task must export, inspect, or prepare a prototype order.

This stage turns the current PCB source into a matching set of files suitable for a fabricator or assembler. Part suitability and allowed substitutions are decided in part selection. Productization and batch release belong to [6.1-production-release.md](6.1-production-release.md).

## Confirm the current candidate

Before export, confirm the current:

- saved PCB source;
- matching schematic, BOM, and board revision;
- outline, layer count, board thickness, copper weight, and major manufacturing requirements;
- DRC result and retained issues;
- bare-board or PCBA scope;
- quantity and assembly variant.

Accepted prototype limitations may remain, but do not mix schematic, PCB, BOM, or placement files from different versions.

If the source is still being edited, save it first and export the complete set again from the same version.

## Generate bare-board files

A bare-board order normally needs:

- Gerber;
- PTH and NPTH drill files;
- slot and routing information;
- board outline;
- necessary fabrication notes.

Check as applicable:

- copper, mask, silkscreen, and mechanical layers are present;
- the outline is closed and correctly sized;
- holes, slots, and internal routing are in the correct files;
- top and bottom orientation is correct;
- copper pours were recalculated;
- mask, stencil, and covered-via requirements match the design;
- the fabricator supports the used width, spacing, drill, and edge clearance;
- no duplicate outline, obsolete layer, or old-version content remains.

File count alone does not prove a correct export.

## Add PCBA files

A PCBA package normally also needs:

- controlled BOM;
- CPL or placement coordinates;
- DNP and assembly variants;
- top and bottom side;
- polarity and pin 1 information;
- necessary assembly notes.

Check the BOM for:

- designators;
- value and role;
- manufacturer and exact MPN, or the defined generic specification;
- package;
- purchasing code selected for this order;
- quantity;
- DNP state;
- whether each substitute is inside an accepted range.

Check the CPL for:

- designators matching the BOM;
- X/Y coordinates;
- top or bottom side;
- rotation;
- origin;
- orientation of polarized and asymmetric parts.

Marketplace identity, EDA package, and purchased part must correspond. A shared marketplace code does not remove the need to check package and orientation.

This stage selects the actual item for this order only from parts or substitution ranges already accepted in [1.2 Part selection and source material](1.2-parts.md). Do not create a new electrical equivalence while ordering.

## Check the manufacturing preview

Use the current board-house or assembly preview to inspect:

- outline, dimensions, and layer count;
- copper and drills;
- slots and unusual geometry;
- mask and silkscreen;
- top and bottom orientation;
- missing parts;
- polarity and pin 1;
- placement rotation;
- DFM messages and automatic board-house changes.

The preview finds export, orientation, and manufacturing-interpretation issues. When it differs from intent, determine whether the source, export configuration, or board-house interpretation owns the error, then correct that source.

## Save the manufacturing candidate

Source, outputs, and review results for one candidate must correspond.

Record at least:

- board name and revision;
- candidate ID or generation time;
- EDA source used;
- Gerber, drill, BOM, and CPL files;
- bare-board or PCBA scope;
- major manufacturing parameters;
- known limits and accepted prototype risks;
- preview or review result.

A project may use:

```text
manufacturing/
└── <candidate-id>/
    ├── manifest.md
    ├── pcb/
    └── pcba/
```

`manifest.md` records only what is needed to pair source, output, and checks. An ordinary prototype does not need a complex release archive.

Do not silently overwrite an accepted candidate with a later export. Generate a new candidate after a design change and repeat the affected checks.

## Prepare the prototype order

Before ordering, confirm:

- the reviewed candidate is selected;
- board-house options match the candidate;
- quantity, thickness, copper, finish, color, and special processes are correct;
- PCBA parts, substitutions, DNP state, side, and quantity are correct;
- quotation or shortage replacements did not introduce an unreviewed part;
- bring-up and prototype tests are defined for arrival.

Generating and checking files is manufacturing preparation. Submitting an order, paying, reserving stock, or deducting inventory creates a new external commitment and requires explicit authorization for that action.

## Finish this stage

The prepared prototype should have:

- a manufacturing candidate paired with current saved source;
- reviewed Gerber and drill files;
- BOM, CPL, and assembly information when needed;
- current board-house preview or equivalent review;
- actual part identities selected for this order;
- known limits and prototype validation items.

For received hardware, continue with [5.1 Prototype bring-up, testing, and revision](5.1-prototype-validation.md).
