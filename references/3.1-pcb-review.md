# 3.1 PCB design and review

Read this reference when the schematic and major parts are stable enough to define the outline, stackup, placement, routing, copper, or DRC.

This stage turns electrical intent into a physical PCB. Manufacturing exports and board-house preview belong to manufacturing preparation.

## Confirm inputs

Read the inputs available for the current PCB. Each may be confirmed, provisional, or not applicable. Return to the prior stage only when a gap prevents useful PCB work or is likely to overturn a major part, package, interface, or outline. Keep other gaps as explicit assumptions and resolve them within PCB work.

Confirm the current:

- schematic and machine Contract;
- parts and exact packages;
- outline, mounting holes, and interface locations;
- enclosure, height, and mechanical limits;
- functional blocks and power tree;
- current, voltage, sensitivity, and topology needs of important nets;
- antenna, acoustic, optical, thermal, and assembly keepouts;
- test points and debugging access that must remain available.

For a provisional item, state which PCB work it limits and when it will be revisited.

If an open issue still changes a major part, package, pin, power structure, or interface, resolve it in schematic work first.

## Establish the PCB foundation

Define:

- outline;
- mounting holes;
- edge connectors;
- mechanical anchors;
- routing and height restrictions;
- copper-layer count;
- role of each layer;
- primary reference planes;
- basic manufacturing capability.

Outline, interfaces, and mounting follow the product and enclosure. Do not move them merely to make routing easier.

## Select the stackup

Choose layer count from the actual design rather than assuming two or four layers.

Consider:

- routing density;
- continuous reference for critical nets;
- power and ground planes;
- current and heat;
- high-speed, RF, analog, or low-noise needs;
- thickness, impedance, and manufacturing capability;
- cost and prototype quantity.

Use the simplest stackup that supports the design. More layers are not automatically better, and fewer layers are not acceptable when they break return paths, routing, or thermal behavior.

Record each layer's main role and the reference used by important signals. Take dielectric thickness, copper weight, and impedance values from the selected board capability or an explicit design assumption.

## Place by functional relationship

Place mechanically constrained objects first:

- connectors;
- switches and buttons;
- sensor openings;
- antennas;
- microphone and speaker interfaces;
- mounting holes;
- debug interfaces;
- indicators and user-visible parts.

Then place circuits by function:

```text
Connector
    → input protection
    → power or interface circuit
    → load or processor
```

Important relationships include:

- protection close to the energy or interference entry;
- decoupling close to its power pins;
- compact switching-power loops;
- feedback and sensing away from switching nodes;
- crystals, references, and sensitive analog paths close to their parts;
- high-current, hot, and noisy circuits away from sensitive blocks;
- real access for probing, insertion, rework, and assembly;
- orientation that respects pin 1, interface direction, cooling, and routing.

The purpose is a clear current and signal flow, not decorative alignment.

Check one important block and the overall partition before expanding to the full placement.

## Reassign pins only when useful

After rough placement, configurable MCU, FPGA, or similar pins may be reconsidered when the current assignment causes an obvious crossing, detour, congestion point, or via.

Use the `unused_configurable_only` policy from [2.1 Schematic design and Contract](2.1-schematic-contract.md). Do not search all permutations or disturb stable interfaces for theoretical optimum routing.

After a real change, update the Contract, schematic, and affected firmware interface before formal routing.

## Define net rules

Use the early electrical intent from 2.1 together with the actual stackup and copper weight.

For important nets, consider:

- continuous and peak current;
- allowed voltage drop;
- working voltage;
- allowed temperature rise;
- route length;
- outer or inner layer;
- via count and geometry;
- return path;
- differential, impedance, or length relationship;
- spacing from noise sources and sensitive nets.

The fabricator's minimum width and clearance are manufacturing limits, not the design value for every net.

A pad escape may be narrower than the trunk, but keep the neckdown short and transition quickly. For high current, inspect the narrowest route, copper, via, and connector segment together.

Do not create complex net classes for every signal. Classify only nets with materially different needs.

## Route in risk order

Route the nets that constrain the rest first:

1. main power and returns;
2. switching-power loops;
3. high-speed, clock, and differential interfaces;
4. feedback, sensing, and sensitive analog;
5. protection and fault-shutdown paths;
6. ordinary control and low-speed digital signals.

For each critical path, inspect:

- actual topology;
- branches and stubs;
- layer transitions;
- reference planes;
- return continuity;
- coupling to nearby noise sources;
- vias and neckdowns;
- whether a test point creates an unnecessary stub.

Automated or bulk routing may handle bounded ordinary remainder nets, but review their actual paths by function afterward.

## Handle ground, planes, and copper

Grounding follows actual current and signal return paths.

Prefer continuous reference. Do not split planes mechanically into analog and digital ground without explaining the current path and how crossing signals return.

Inspect:

- decoupling loops;
- power-device and thermal-pad ground;
- ESD discharge path;
- reference return after signal layer changes;
- isolated copper;
- narrow channels;
- returns cut by keepouts or slots;
- the copper that remains after pouring.

Ground vias serve a real return, thermal, or shielding purpose. Via count and regular grids are not goals.

For EasyEDA Pro:

- stackup, outline, keepouts, and real copper:
  [3.2-pcb-foundation.md](providers/easyeda-pro/3.2-pcb-foundation.md);
- layer-transition returns and ground vias:
  [3.3-pcb-grounding.md](providers/easyeda-pro/3.3-pcb-grounding.md).

The actual sequence is: use 3.2 for layers, outline, and functional keepouts; return here for placement and critical routing; revisit 3.2 to repour and confirm reference copper; then use 3.3 for return paths and grounding before the final whole-board review here.

## Handle thermal and mechanical conditions

Check:

- copper area and heat path for power parts;
- thermal pads and vias;
- heat transferred to sensors, batteries, and enclosure;
- airflow and sealed-enclosure behavior;
- component height and assembly orientation;
- connector insertion clearance;
- screws, standoffs, and enclosure loads;
- probe and rework access;
- acoustic ports, optical paths, airflow, and antenna regions.

An empty area may be functional for RF, acoustics, cooling, assembly, or routing. It does not need to be filled for visual density.

## Review the board

After placement and routing, check:

- remaining unrouted nets;
- actual net-class assignment;
- critical width, clearance, via, and topology;
- power-path neckdowns;
- differential and impedance requirements;
- reference planes and transition returns;
- pours, islands, and unintended disconnects;
- edge, hole, and slot clearance;
- pads, solder-mask bridges, and stencil needs;
- pin 1, polarity, and component orientation;
- silkscreen over pads or hidden under parts;
- top and bottom orientation;
- mechanical collision, height, and insertion space;
- accessible test points.

Run the project's configured DRC and inspect the actual rules and remaining issues.

DRC proves only geometry covered by those rules. It does not replace power, return-path, thermal, mechanical, or functional review.

## Finish PCB work

Before manufacturing preparation, confirm:

- the PCB source is saved;
- major parts, outline, holes, and interfaces are correct;
- all required nets are routed;
- critical power, signal, and return paths match the design;
- actual copper is current;
- pin changes were synchronized to the schematic and Contract;
- DRC has no unexplained current issue;
- mechanical, assembly, test, and thermal conditions were reviewed.

Handle Gerber, drill, BOM, CPL, and board-house preview in [4.1-production-handoff.md](4.1-production-handoff.md).
